About Me

I'm an undergraduate in Computer Science and Engineering at Delhi Technological University (2025–2029). My research is on LLM safety: how language models get jailbroken, especially through multi-turn and persona attacks, and cheap defenses that work without retraining the model. My current work asks whether simply warning a frozen model that a prompt looks like a known attack is enough to make it refuse, and where that protection breaks down.

I also work on security for autonomous systems. I built a software-only GNSS spoofing testbed for PX4 drones and a LiDAR-based detector that notices the spoof and moves the drone off GPS. And I build retrieval-grounded LLM apps, such as DTUnator, a course assistant that refuses to answer rather than hallucinate.

I'm looking for AI research internships and fellowships, particularly in AI safety and alignment.

Research

Does Warning Help? On Alerting Language Models to Jailbreaks Before They Act
Keshav Talwar et al. · Under review FLMSec @ NeurIPS 2026 (workshop)
  • A training-free defense: remember the attacks that have beaten a frozen model, each with a short teacher-written note on how it works. When a new prompt is close to one of them, put a warning in front of it.
  • Attack-success rate falls from 0.40 to 0.24 across five public jailbreak datasets, while benign over-refusal drops from 0.13 to 0.10.
  • The protection is conditional: it fades as the retrieval threshold tightens, and a standing system-prompt warning influences the model but doesn't bind it under persona attacks.

Multi-Turn Jailbreak Defense & Lifelong Safety Alignment
Independent research · 2026
  • A guard against Crescendo attacks (harmless-looking turns that escalate together): conversation memory, an escalation scorer and a fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier in front of a local Llama-3.2-3B.
  • An attacker vs defender arms race: an LLM attacker mines strategies from jailbreak papers and attacks a defender; a judge scores each attempt, and successful attacks go into a FAISS attack memory for retraining.
  • Re-implemented a stateful multi-turn detector that scores each new turn with one recurrent step instead of re-reading the whole conversation.

GPS Spoofing Attacks & LiDAR-Based Detection for Autonomous Drones
Simulation research · PX4 SITL + Gazebo · 2025–present · software only, no RF
  • Signal-level spoofing (gps-sdr-sim → gnss-sdr → PX4): the fake signal captures the receiver at ≥ +2 dB, moving the fix about 420 m from truth. A walk-off variant drags it 300 m at 3.75 m/s with no visible jump.
  • Detection and recovery: compares KISS-ICP LiDAR odometry with the GPS-aided estimate. When they disagree by more than 2 m, it switches PX4's EKF2 from GPS to LiDAR.
  • Autonomy stack: A* + VFH+ obstacle avoidance, hybrid ORB-SLAM3/KISS-ICP odometry, a web mission planner, and a real-terrain world of Prayagraj.
